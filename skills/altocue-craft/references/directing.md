# Directing: getting footage the edit can use

Most problems an editor cannot fix were made before the camera rolled. When the user has not filmed yet, or can film again, this page is what to tell them. It is written for one person with a phone or one camera, and it scales up. Lines here are [thumb] unless labelled. What each shot size means is in [shots](shots.md).

## Before the shoot: the script

- **One sentence first.** Who the film is for, what it shows them, and why they should care. See [structure](structure.md).
- **Break the script into chunks of two or three sentences.** [source] Chris Lavigne at Wistia. A person who is not an actor can hold a short thought and say it as their own.
- **Mark the lines that will be covered by inserts.** [source] Lavigne. Those can be read from the page. It takes pressure off the presenter and tells the edit where cutaways are planned.
- **Write three openings.** The opening is the part most likely to be changed in the edit, so film choices.
- **Read it aloud once, away from the camera.** Fix what trips the tongue. Notes on energy and pronunciation land better before the lights are on.

## Coverage: give the edit somewhere to go

- **Two sizes of the presenter.** Film at the highest resolution the camera has, so a tighter size can be cut from the same take. Or say the script twice at two sizes.
- **Shoot what is named.** Every thing the script names and every action it describes is a possible insert. Shoot the real screen, the real product and the real result first: proof beats illustration.
- **Shoot actions as a sequence of five.** [source] Michael Rosenblum's five-shot method: the hands close, the face, a wide of the place, over the shoulder, and one unexpected angle. Five steady shots cut into a sequence that a single wide never gives.
- **Three sizes of every insert.** Wide, medium and close, each held for about ten seconds with the camera still.
- **Overlap the action.** Start each shot before the move and run past its end. Those are the handles a cut on movement needs.
- **Shoot the place.** One establishing shot and two details, for openings and section breaks.
- **Shoot the listening.** In a conversation, a reaction is both a cut point and a cover.
- **Record the room.** Ten seconds of silence in the same position, with everyone still. It fills every gap the edit opens.

## The frame

- **Lens at eye height.** Looking down on a speaker shrinks them, and looking up puts the ceiling in.
- **Eyes about a third of the way down the frame,** with less space above the head as the shot gets closer.
- **Space in front of a look.** A head turned to one side sits off centre with room ahead of it. A speaker addressing the lens sits on the centre line.
- **Leave room for text.** For vertical video, keep the face out of the bottom third and the far top, where apps draw over the picture.
- **Distance from the wall.** Pull the speaker away from the background and angle the camera to the walls. [source] Jay Holben in American Cinematographer. It gives depth, and lines that lead to the face.
- **A phone's main camera is a wide lens.** Close to a face it distorts. Keep it at arm's length or more, or use the longer camera and step back.
- **Lock it off.** A tripod, a shelf, a stack of books. Movement should be a choice.
- **Lock exposure, focus and white balance.** A camera that adjusts during a take gives the grade a moving target.

## Light

- **One large soft light, to one side and a little above.** A window is the best one most people own. [source] Holben: place the key so the shadowed side of the face is towards the lens. That gives the face shape.
- **Face the window, do not sit in front of it.** A bright window behind the speaker turns them into a shadow.
- **One colour of light.** Daylight and a warm lamp together give two whites, and no correction fixes both. Turn one off.
- **A light in the eyes.** A small reflection in each eye makes a face look alive.
- **The background a little darker, and a little cooler, than the face.** [source] Holben. Separation in brightness and colour reads as depth.
- **Check what moves.** People and traffic through a window distract, and passing clouds change the exposure halfway through a take.

## Sound on the day

- **Microphone close.** The single biggest difference between amateur and professional sound is distance. A clip-on or a small microphone a hand's width from the mouth beats any camera microphone across a room.
- **Kill the room.** Turn off fans, fridges and notifications. Soft furnishings help. A bare room rings, and echo is the fault that clean-up handles worst.
- **Watch the level.** Loud laughs and plosives clip. Clipped sound cannot be rebuilt.
- **Water within reach.** A dry mouth clicks.
- **Clap once at the start** if sound and picture are recorded on two devices.

## Directing someone who is not an actor

Lines marked [source] are Chris Lavigne's working method at Wistia.

- **Clear the room.** Director and presenter only. An audience makes people perform. [source]
- **Have everything ready before they arrive.** Your attention belongs on them. [source]
- **Give them a person, not a lens.** "Tell it to a friend who is about to make this mistake." People speak well to someone and badly to a camera.
- **Direct the intention, never the reading.** Say "warn them" or "let them in on it". An imitation of your reading sounds like an imitation.
- **Skip the prompter where you can.** [source] Lines that are known sound more like the person. If one is needed, put it on the lens axis and far enough away that the eyes do not scan.
- **Say before you start that there will be several takes,** and that slips are fixed in the edit. [source] A presenter who thinks each take must be perfect tightens up.
- **One note per take, and keep it short.** [source]
- **Ask for one take far too big.** [source] The next take lands at the right size.
- **After a good take, ask for one more for safety.** [source] Relief makes the next one better.
- **Redo the opening lines at the end.** [source] They are relaxed by then, and the opening matters most.
- **When they are stuck, get them to move.** [source] Shake it out, and do it with them.
- **Stand them up.** Energy drops in a chair. If seated, sit on the front half.
- **Start and end clean.** Breathe, look at the lens, hold for two seconds, speak. At the end, hold the look for two seconds. Those holds are the edit's handles.
- **Say a retake aloud.** Stop, then say the whole sentence again from its start. Altocue hears a restart as a retake and keeps the better one. Never patch half a sentence.

For the edit this gives three rules: prefer later takes of early lines, expect the first minutes of a session to be the stiffest, and judge lines marked for inserts by sound alone.

## Interviews

- **Eyes just beside the lens, with the interviewer's head close to it.** [source] Holben. The closer the eyeline is to the lens, the more of both eyes the viewer sees.
- **Several people in one film: alternate the side they look to.** [source] Holben. Speakers who face each other across cuts feel like a conversation.
- **Ask open questions and stay silent after the answer.** People fill a silence with the better line.
- **Ask them to put the question into the answer,** so the answer stands alone when the question is cut.
- **Do not talk over them.** A murmur of agreement under their best line cannot be removed.

## Movement and props

- **A move marks a new section.** A step to a second mark, a sit, a turn to a screen. The new position is a new frame, so the cut has a reason.
- **Motivate every move.** Go to the thing being named, lean in on the point. Unmotivated movement reads as nerves.
- **Hands inside the frame and below the face.** A gesture across the mouth spoils the take.
- **Hold a prop still for two seconds,** at chest height, tilted away from reflections, so a close shot can be taken.
- **Mark the floor.** A presenter who returns to the same mark gives takes that cut together.

## The shot list

Write it from the script, not from the location. One row per shot:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| What happens | She plugs in the drive and the light turns green |
| Size and angle | Close, over the shoulder |
| Move | Locked off |
| Covers which line | "It backs up the moment you plug it in" |
| Hold | Ten seconds, with the action twice |
| Must have | Yes |

- **Order it by setup.** Group everything that shares a camera position and light.
- **Shoot the must haves first.**
- **Name the files or leave a note,** so the insert search can find each shot by what it shows.

## A pickup list

When the edit shows something missing, ask for the smallest thing that fixes it. A pickup request names the exact line to say or the exact thing to show, the size, where to look, and what it will join to. "Say this one sentence again, same shirt, same seat, looking at the lens" is a request someone can do in five minutes.
