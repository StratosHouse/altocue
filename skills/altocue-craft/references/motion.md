# Motion: text and graphics that earn their place

A graphic is a second voice. It should say one thing, at the moment the first voice needs help, and then leave. Lines here are [thumb] unless labelled. How to write each graphic is in `altocue-motion`, and the house builds are in its studio graphics reference.

## Does this moment need a graphic?

A graphic earns its place when it does one of these:

1. **Names** someone or something the viewer needs to hold on to.
2. **Proves** a claim: a number, a quote, a screen, a logo.
3. **Maps** a structure: step two of three, this against that.
4. **Covers** a fault in the picture: a look away, a jump.
5. **Marks** a turn: a new section, the ending.

If none applies, leave the frame alone. A film with a graphic on every line has no emphasis left. Decide what the three most important moments are and spend the design there.

## One idea at a time

- **One message per screen.** Two graphics at once are read as neither.
- **Text says what the speaker says, when they say it.** Never ahead of them: a viewer who reads the punchline early stops listening. Never a different claim.
- **Fewer words than the speech.** A graphic is the headline of the sentence, not its transcript. Captions are the transcript.
- **A number needs its unit and its subject.** "41%" alone is decoration. "41% of runway" is information.
- **Show enough to feel like the number.** If the line says twelve tools, three icons do not carry it.

## Type

- **Big enough for a phone held at arm's length.** Read every graphic at about 360 pixels wide without zooming. If you squint, it is too small.
- **Two sizes are a hierarchy. Four are noise.** One headline size, one supporting size.
- **Two weights at most,** and one typeface family unless the brand has two.
- **Short lines.** Break by phrase, never leaving one word alone on the last line, and never splitting a name, a number from its unit, or an article from its noun.
- **Contrast against the picture, not against a guess.** Text over footage needs a scrim, a card or a shadow that holds on the brightest frame under it.
- **Sentence case reads fastest.** Capitals are for a few short words.
- **Emphasise one phrase.** A highlight on everything is a highlight on nothing.

## Layout

- **Inside the safe zone.** Apps draw buttons, names and captions over the edges, and each app's zone differs. The `safe_zone` gate measures it. Look anyway.
- **Never over the face.** Eyes and mouth are what the viewer came for. The `face_clear` gate measures it.
- **Leave margins.** Cards do not touch the frame edge or each other.
- **Align to something.** A shared left edge or a shared centre line across every graphic in the film makes them one system.
- **Put the next thing where the eye already is.** After a graphic leaves, the eye is still on that spot for a moment.
- **Graphics and captions share the screen by plan.** When a full graphic is up, captions move or rest.

## Timing

- **On the word.** A graphic that names a thing lands as the word is said, or a few frames before. Late reads as a mistake.
- **Long enough to read twice.** Read the text at a calm pace, twice. That is the hold. A graphic that leaves early is the most common complaint about machine-made video.
- **Gone before the speaker has moved on.** A stat from the last point still on screen during the next one fights it.
- **Rest between graphics.** Leave a breath of plain picture between one leaving and the next arriving, unless they are a deliberate sequence.
- **Land on joins.** A graphic arriving on a cut hides the cut and makes both feel intended.
- **The end card is short.** Long enough to read the action, no longer.

## Movement

- **Movement says where something came from and how heavy it is.** Nothing should move for no reason.
- **Ease everything.** Things in the world speed up and slow down. Arrivals decelerate into place. Exits accelerate away. Linear movement reads as mechanical.
- **Arrive quickly, leave quicker.** An entrance of roughly a third of a second and a shorter exit keeps the film moving. Large elements travelling far need a little longer.
- **Overshoot is seasoning.** A small settle past the mark gives life to a playful brand. On a serious one it is wrong.
- **Stagger a group.** Items that arrive a few frames apart, in reading order, are read in that order. Items that arrive together are read as one block.
- **One direction per film.** If things enter from the left, they keep entering from the left, unless direction means something.
- **Still while being read.** Text that is moving is not being read. Animate in, hold still, animate out.
- **Animate the change, not the object.** For a count, a chart or a before and after, the thing that changed is what moves.

## Depth and finish

- **A soft shadow or a faint edge lifts a card off footage.** Enough to separate it, not enough to be noticed.
- **Blur and glow in small amounts.** A background blurred behind a card makes the card readable. Glow on everything is a filter.
- **Match the footage.** Graphics sharper, more saturated and cleaner than the picture look pasted on. Grain and the film's look go over the graphics too.
- **Motion blur on fast moves** makes them feel filmed. Without it a fast slide strobes.

## Kinds of graphic and when each one helps

| Graphic | Use it when | Avoid it when |
|---|---|---|
| Hook title | The opening line needs to be read as well as heard | It says something different from the spoken line |
| Lower third | A person appears for the first time | They have already been named |
| Chip or label | A list item or a term needs a handle | Every sentence gets one |
| Stat | A number is the proof | The number was not said or supplied |
| Quote card | Someone's exact words matter | The words are paraphrased |
| Steps | The viewer must follow an order | There are more steps than fit on one screen |
| Chart | A comparison or a change over time is the point | One number would do |
| Screen or demo | The product doing the thing is the proof | The action shown would not happen in that order |
| Logo | A named tool or brand is mentioned | The user has no right to show it |
| Full-screen card | The picture underneath must be covered | It leaves the speaker silent on screen afterwards |
| End card | There is one action to take | There are two |

## Graphics and sound

Every visible event may carry one sound, and most small ones should carry none. See [sound](sound.md) for which sound meets which event and on which frame.

## Brand

- **The brand kit comes first.** Colours, typefaces and the logo are read with `altocue brand` before any graphic is made.
- **Two brand colours and a neutral are enough.** One for emphasis, one for structure.
- **A client's film wears the client's brand,** not the editor's.
- **Consistency is the design.** The same card, the same spacing and the same movement through the film reads as craft. A new style for each graphic reads as a template pack.

## Truth

- **Every number, name, logo, quote and review comes from the user or the speech.**
- **A demo shows what the product really does,** in the order a user would do it.
- **A placeholder never ships.** If the real figure is missing, show no figure and ask.

## Checking graphics without playback

Use the graphics sheets from `altocue watch`:

- **Spelling, names and numbers** against the transcript.
- **Phone size.** Shrink the frame and read it.
- **The frame under the text.** Is the contrast held on the brightest frame of the hold?
- **Start and end.** Does it arrive on its word, and is it gone before the next idea?
- **The frames either side.** No half-drawn graphic on the first or last frame of a shot.

A still cannot show easing, stutter or flicker. Say so, and name the moves a person should watch.
