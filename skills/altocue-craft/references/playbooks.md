# Playbooks: how each kind of video is built

A kind of video is a set of promises to the viewer about pace, picture and length. Pick the kind before the first cut, because it decides almost every setting that follows. Each playbook gives the job, the shape, the pace, the picture, the sound, the text and the ending. Lines here are [thumb] unless labelled: they are an editor's starting points, never a forecast of how a film will do.

Mixed footage still gets one kind. A podcast cut into a vertical clip is a short, not a podcast.

## Short talking video (vertical, under ninety seconds)

**The job.** One idea, delivered to someone who did not ask for it and can leave with a thumb.

- **Shape.** Claim or result first, then the proof, then the payoff, then one action. No greeting, no name, no "so today". See [structure](structure.md).
- **Pace.** Tight. Pauses inside a clause go. Pauses after a full stop stay short but real. One held beat before the key line.
- **Picture.** The subject in the first frame. A change of size, an insert or a graphic when the idea turns, and never on a clock. A punch-in is a statement, so save it for the claim, the number and the payoff.
- **Sound.** Voice forward. Music, if any, well under the voice and ending on purpose. One effect per visible event, none on a word.
- **Text.** Captions on, short pages, inside the app's safe zone. A title in the first second that agrees with the spoken line.
- **Ending.** The last useful word, a beat, out. If it may loop, the last line hands back to the first.
- **Check first.** The opening sheet from `altocue watch`: would a stranger know what this is by three seconds?

## Long talking video (horizontal, minutes)

**The job.** Hold someone who chose to watch, for as long as the subject deserves and no longer.

- **Shape.** Cold open on the strongest moment, a short title, one line on the route, then chapters. Each chapter has its own small promise and payoff, and ends by naming the question the next one answers.
- **Pace.** Natural. Leave the speaker's cadence alone and remove what stalls: restarts, repeats, side roads. Cut sentences before you shave gaps.
- **Picture.** Two sizes of the speaker are enough. Go wide for a new chapter, tight for the point. Cover a long explanation with what it describes: a screen, a diagram, a clip.
- **Sound.** Level from line to line matters more than anything else across minutes. Music only at the open, at chapter turns and at the end.
- **Text.** Chapter titles, names, numbers, terms the viewer may not know. Full captions are a delivery file here, not a design layer.
- **Ending.** The point in the speaker's best words, one action, stop. No recap of the recap.
- **Check first.** `script.md` read top to bottom: does each chapter cause the next?

## Tutorial and screen recording

**The job.** The viewer can do the thing afterwards.

- **Shape.** Show the finished result first, then the steps in the order a beginner must do them, then the result again.
- **Pace.** Fast through waiting, typing and loading. Slow at each decision. A step the viewer must copy stays on screen long enough to copy.
- **Picture.** The screen is the subject. Punch in on the part of the screen being discussed so it is readable at phone size, and return wide when the place in the interface changes. The speaker's face is an insert, not the base.
- **Sound.** Voice dry and close. Music low or absent: it competes with concentration. A soft click on a real click is enough.
- **Text.** Step numbers, key names, the exact value to type. Never a paragraph. The screen recording keeps its true colour: a look that shifts interface colours is a fault.
- **Ending.** The result, where to get the files, stop.
- **Check first.** Every sheet at phone size: can each control named in the words be read?

## Interview and conversation

**The job.** Let one person be understood, with the other as the viewer's stand-in.

- **Shape.** Question as asked, the short answer, the reason, the caveat. Across a whole piece, order the answers so each raises the next question.
- **Pace.** Relaxed. A pause before an answer is thinking, and thinking is the content. Reactions such as "mm" and "ah" are often the moment. Never cut the caveat off an answer.
- **Picture.** Cut to the listener to compress an answer, not to decorate it. Hold on the speaker through the important line. Keep both people on their own side of the frame so the eyelines meet.
- **Sound.** Each voice at its own steady level. Overlaps are real: keep the ones that carry agreement or laughter, lose the ones that bury a word.
- **Text.** A name and role on first appearance. A pulled quote only if it is word for word.
- **Ending.** The answer that says the most, left alone.
- **Check first.** The truth pass in [the passes](passes.md): does every trimmed answer still mean what was said?

## Clip from a long recording

**The job.** One self-contained moment that makes sense to someone who never saw the rest.

- **Shape.** Find the payoff line first, then walk backwards to the earliest line it needs. Anything before that is context the clip must do without or state in a title.
- **Pace.** As the short talking video, but keep the speaker's breath before the key line.
- **Picture.** Reframe on whoever is speaking. A title that supplies the missing context in one line.
- **Sound.** Start clean on a full word and end on a finished one. Laughter or a reaction after the payoff can be the ending.
- **Text.** Captions on. The source named if the clip will travel.
- **Ending.** The payoff and its reaction. No outro.
- **Check first.** Every "this", "that" and "as I said" points at something inside the clip.

## Product demo and launch film

**The job.** The viewer sees the product do the thing, and believes it.

- **Shape.** The problem in one picture, the product doing the work, the result, where to get it. Show each claim happening. A feature that is only named is a claim without proof.
- **Pace.** Builds. Short shots through the features, one held shot on the result.
- **Picture.** Real interface and real output. A demo action must make sense in the order a user would do it. Logos appear when named. No empty frames.
- **Sound.** Music drives it, so cuts may land on the beat where no one is speaking. Effects on real events: a click, a render finishing, a card landing.
- **Text.** Few words, large, one idea at a time. Every number comes from the user or the product.
- **Ending.** Name, one line of what it is, where to get it. A short end card.
- **Check first.** The graphics sheets: is each one on screen long enough to read twice?

## Advert and creator-style product video

**The job.** A specific person recognises their own problem and takes one step.

- **Shape.** The viewer's problem in their words, the turn, the product in use, the proof, the offer, one action.
- **Pace.** Tight, with one honest pause at the turn.
- **Picture.** The product in a hand, in a room, in use. Close when the detail matters. One location is enough.
- **Sound.** A natural voice in a natural room reads as true. Polish that removes the room removes the trust.
- **Text.** The offer exactly as given. A disclosure label where the rules ask for one. See `altocue-ugc`.
- **Ending.** The action, once.
- **Check first.** Every claim is one the brand made and can stand behind.

## Testimonial

**The job.** A real person vouches, in their own way of speaking.

- **Shape.** Life before, the change, life after. The story shape in [structure](structure.md).
- **Pace.** Natural to relaxed. Keep hesitations that carry feeling. A testimonial cut too clean sounds written.
- **Picture.** Eye level, steady, medium close. Inserts of the thing they describe, never stock that pretends to be their life.
- **Sound.** Their voice, their room, quiet music under the last third if any.
- **Text.** Name and what they are to the product. Nothing they did not say.
- **Ending.** Their strongest sentence, then a held look.
- **Check first.** Consent and accuracy: is this what they meant?

## Event recap and montage

**The job.** The feeling of having been there.

- **Shape.** Arrival, build, peak, afterglow. Time order is optional. Energy order is not.
- **Pace.** Set by the music. Cut on the beat, on movement, and hold the peak.
- **Picture.** Wide to place, medium for people, close for detail. Vary size at every cut. Match direction of movement across cuts.
- **Sound.** Music first, with moments of real sound lifted through it: applause, a line from the stage, a laugh.
- **Text.** Name, place, date. Little else.
- **Ending.** On the downbeat of the last phrase of the music, or on real sound after the music stops.
- **Check first.** The cuts sheets: is there a real change of size or subject at every cut?

## Story and documentary piece

**The job.** The viewer cares what happens to someone.

- **Shape.** A person who wants something, what stands in the way, the turn, what it cost, what it means.
- **Pace.** Varies most of all the kinds. Quick through setup, slow at the turn, silent after the hardest line.
- **Picture.** Let shots run. Motivated cutaways only: what they are looking at, what they are talking about.
- **Sound.** Room sound is a character. Music enters late and leaves early.
- **Text.** Time and place, names. No commentary in titles.
- **Ending.** An image, held, that answers the opening one.
- **Check first.** The honesty rules in [structure](structure.md): has the order of true lines created a claim nobody made?

## Choosing between two kinds

- **Who chose to watch?** A viewer who searched will give minutes. A viewer who was shown the film gives seconds. Build for the second unless you know it is the first.
- **What is the subject?** A face, a screen, a product, an event. The subject fills the base layer, and everything else is an insert.
- **Where does it play?** The place decides the aspect, the safe zone and whether sound can be assumed.
- **What footage exists?** A kind that needs proof shots the user does not have is the wrong kind. Say so, and offer the nearest kind the footage can carry or a short list of pickups to film.

## One film, several versions

A horizontal long video, three vertical clips from it and a square advert are three kinds from one recording. Cut each from the selects as its own film, with its own opening line and its own ending. A long video cropped to vertical is not a short.
