# Story craft for short-form character reels

Nothing in this file predicts how a film will do. Each claim carries a label, so you can tell how much weight it bears:

- **[evidence]** a study or a hard fact, with its limits stated.
- **[source]** someone said so, and nothing here tested it. Treat it as a lead.
- **[thumb]** a rule of thumb from the trade. Useful, not measured.

The six shapes, the hook list and the rules below are [thumb] unless marked. Their timings are starting points.

## What is known, and what is not

- **[evidence] Prediction before publishing is bounded.** In a controlled test with 14,341 people, the best songs rarely flopped and the worst rarely won, and anything else could happen (Salganik et al., Science 2006, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1121066)). That was music, not reels.
- **[evidence] People leave early, and often stay.** Across 9.2 million TikTok recommendations to 347 users, 45% of views ran to the end and 24% skipped before 20% of the video (Zannettou et al., CHI 2024, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642433)). A convenience sample, and it says nothing about edit choices.
- **[evidence] Faster is not simply better.** Fast pace raised attention, but fast pace with arousing content overloaded viewers and lowered recall (Lang et al., 1999, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/08838159909364504)). Television, and old. Measure cuts per 10 seconds with `altocue facts`. Never treat it as a target.
- **[evidence] Captions help understanding.** Over 100 studies found better comprehension, attention and memory (Gernsbacher, 2015, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/2372732215602130)). This is about understanding, not about keeping viewers.
- **[evidence] Who makes it often matters more than how it is cut.** On 5.3 million YouTube videos, context, topic and channel explained most of the variation in engagement (Wu et al., ICWSM 2018, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v12i1.15031)). Long-form, from 2016.
- **[thumb] Make a reel work with no sound.** Do it for clarity and for people who cannot hear it. The old figure of 80 to 85% of Reels playing muted has no source for Reels: it comes from Facebook feed video, and secondhand figures for Reels point the other way, at about 75 to 90% sound on. No primary source was found either way.
- **[thumb] Get to the point at once.** The figures usually quoted for this, 71% of viewers deciding in the opening seconds and a 1.5 second distribution decision, have no traceable source. They are not used here.
- **[source] Loops are said to lift completion.** No study was found. YouTube leaves looping traffic out of average view duration and engaged views, so a loop may earn nothing there.
- **[source] Brand-character work is said to do best when the brand barely appears.** The target comment is "wait, this is an ad?". No numbers are given, because none were traceable.

## The six story shapes

Pick ONE shape per reel. A reel that explains two things explains nothing at
silent-scroll speed.

1. **Overwhelm-to-Exhale (12-20s).** 0-1.5s chaos already in motion; 2-8s
   escalation in 2-3 cuts; 8-14s the turn (one small intervention, often a
   second character or the world itself); 14-18s the EXHALE, held 2 full
   seconds of stillness after motion. For mental-load validation.
2. **Two-Hands Handoff (8-15s).** One character mid-struggle with a
   two-character task; the second pair of hands arrives WITHOUT being asked;
   the task turns easy, almost musical. Close on the two characters, not the
   task. For both-parent accountability; alternate who carries and who
   arrives.
3. **Five Small Things (15-25s).** Countable ritual: five beats ~3s each,
   visibly counted (lanterns, pebbles, petals lighting one by one), then the
   wide showing five small things made one good day.
4. **Hard Day That Counts (12-20s).** A plan visibly fails; the character
   sits in it (do NOT rescue too fast; let the sad beat breathe); then
   reframe, not fix: the day still gets the identical mark as a good day.
   Close on the mark, not a smile.
5. **Invisible List Revealed (10-18s).** Mundane action; pull back / overlay
   reveals everything silently tracked (threads, a hidden ledger); a second
   character SEES it for the first time; hold the reaction.
6. **Seamless Loop (8-12s).** A cyclical ritual where the last frame IS the
   first. Direct the ending first so it match-cuts into the opening. For
   ambient world content. `altocue facts` measures how close the last frame
   is to the first.

## Hook taxonomy (no talking heads)

**[source]** The visual outcome drop-in is often named the strongest hook:
zero setup, the payoff already in frame one. Nobody here has tested that.

- **Mid-action drop-in**: open inside the moment; never establish first.
- **Wrong-scale object**: tiny character, absurdly big list/pile. Instantly
  legible with no sound; says "mental load" without a word.
- **POV text overlay**: "POV: you're the parent who remembered the water
  bottle." Casts the viewer as the character.
- **Confession card**: one specific, slightly raw truth as text ("nobody
  claps when you remember the snack"); the scene answers it. Specific beats
  clever.
- **Pattern break**: one discordant element in the soft world (rain indoors,
  one item out of place); resolve it at the end (open loop).
- **Count promise**: "5 things" / "day 12" on frame one. **[source]** Serial
  formats are said to earn more engagement than standalone posts. The usual
  figure has no traceable source, so it is not repeated here.

Never open with: a logo, an establishing wide, a character waving hello, or
any "let me tell you about" equivalent.

## Concept-without-product rules

1. Dramatize the RELIEF, not the mechanism (sell the exhale, not the
   checklist).
2. The concept appears as PHYSICS of the world: magic that behaves the way
   the product behaves.
3. One concept per reel.
4. Fixed character registers: dynamics stay constant across every reel so
   accountability stories write themselves as relationship beats. Bend the
   trend to the character, never the character to the trend.

## Never do

- Guilt framing. Many parents already carry it, and backlash is real. Failure
  is weather, never character flaw.
- "Enjoy it while it lasts" sentimentality (reads as a threat).
- Perfect-parent contrast (no honor roll in the world).
- App UI, phones, notification sounds inside the story world.
- Mom-only framing of care work.
- Engagement-bait CTAs ("comment YES", "tag a mom who..."). **[source]**
  Platforms are said to suppress these.
- Breaking the character's emotional register for a trend.

## Loop, caption, CTA mechanics

- **Text overlays**: max one line at a time; high contrast; safe zones; the
  reel works with zero audio. **[source]** QA holds graphic text to 3 words a
  second, the top of the 160 to 180 words a minute Ofcom recommends for
  subtitles. That is a subtitle guideline, not a phone study. **[evidence]** In
  BBC R&D's 2015 tests, about 175 words a minute felt right and about 230 too
  fast, and viewers accepted real clips far past the guideline when the
  subtitles matched the speech, so spoken captions are left out of the gate.
  **[thumb]** About 3 seconds a card.
- **Caption is the second act**: the video validates, the caption converts
  quietly. Formula: one resonant line extending the story + one soft
  identity line + searchable keywords ("mental load", "default parent",
  "parenting as a team").
- **CTA norms**: open-ended questions are safe ("what counted today?");
  route traffic via profile and a keyword-rich pinned comment; keep most
  reels CTA-free. Being sought out beats asking.
- **Seed the search**: end some reels on an unresolved detail so comments
  ask "what is this from?"; answer in character.
