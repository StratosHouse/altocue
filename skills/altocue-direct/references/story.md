# Story craft for short films

Nothing in this file predicts how a film will do. Each claim carries a label, so you can tell how much weight it bears:

- **[evidence]** a study, a published standard, a platform's own written rule or a hard fact, with its limits stated.
- **[source]** someone said so, and nothing here tested it. Treat it as a lead. A small count by one author stays here too.
- **[thumb]** a rule of thumb from the trade. Useful, not measured.

The shapes, the hook list and the rules below are [thumb] unless marked. Their timings are starting points.

## What is known, and what is not

- **[evidence] Prediction before publishing is bounded.** In a controlled test with 14,341 people, the best songs rarely flopped and the worst rarely won, and anything else could happen (Salganik et al., Science 2006, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1121066)). That was music, not reels.
- **[evidence] People leave early, and often stay.** Across 9.2 million TikTok recommendations to 347 users, 45% of views ran to the end and 24% skipped before 20% of the video (Zannettou et al., CHI 2024, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642433)). A convenience sample, and it says nothing about edit choices.
- **[evidence] Faster is not simply better.** Fast pace raised attention, but fast pace with arousing content overloaded viewers and lowered recall (Lang et al., 1999, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/08838159909364504)). Television, and old. Measure cuts per 10 seconds with `altocue facts`. Never treat it as a target.
- **[evidence] Captions help understanding.** Over 100 studies found better comprehension, attention and memory (Gernsbacher, 2015, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/2372732215602130)). This is about understanding, not about keeping viewers.
- **[evidence] Who makes it often matters more than how it is cut.** On 5.3 million YouTube videos, context, topic and channel explained most of the variation in engagement (Wu et al., ICWSM 2018, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v12i1.15031)). Long-form, from 2016.
- **[thumb] Make a reel work with no sound.** Do it for clarity and for people who cannot hear it. The old figure of 80 to 85% of Reels playing muted has no source for Reels: it comes from Facebook feed video, and secondhand figures for Reels point the other way, at about 75 to 90% sound on. No primary source was found either way.
- **[thumb] Get to the point at once.** The figures usually quoted for this, 71% of viewers deciding in the opening seconds and a 1.5 second distribution decision, have no traceable source. They are not used here.
- **[source] Loops are said to lift completion.** No study was found. YouTube leaves looping traffic out of average view duration and engaged views, so a loop may earn nothing there.
- **[source] Brand-character work is said to do best when the brand barely appears.** The target comment is "wait, this is an ad?". No numbers are given, because none were traceable.

## Story shapes

Pick ONE shape per film. A reel that explains two things explains nothing at scroll speed.

1. **Overwhelm to exhale (12-20s).** Chaos already in motion in the first second. Escalate in two or three cuts. Then the turn: one small intervention. Hold two full seconds of stillness after the motion.
2. **Two-hands handoff (8-15s).** One character mid-struggle with a two-person task. A second pair of hands arrives without being asked. The task turns easy. Close on the two characters, not the task.
3. **Five small things (15-25s).** A countable ritual: five beats, visibly counted, then a wide showing they made one thing.
4. **Hard day that counts (12-20s).** A plan visibly fails. The character sits in it, and nobody rescues too fast. Then reframe rather than fix. Close on the mark, not a smile.
5. **Invisible list revealed (10-18s).** A mundane action. Pull back to reveal everything silently tracked. A second character sees it for the first time. Hold the reaction.
6. **Seamless loop (8-12s).** A cyclical ritual where the last frame is the first. Direct the ending first, so it match-cuts into the opening. `altocue facts` measures how close the last frame is to the first.
7. **State table.** Use it when a value carries the story, such as a balance, a dose or a score. **[source]** Write the table of states before any line: each state, its value, what changes it. Narration follows at one or two lines a state, and the number is spoken on the same cut where the display changes. Any value exaggerated for reading speed says so on screen.
8. **Ladder.** A counter climbs a few rungs, and each rung holds one feeling, one real fact and one literal picture. **[source]** It needs a checkable mechanism. **[evidence]** Never turn it into a series where one character meets the same outcome each time: YouTube names that as not monetisable. See [the platform rules](../../altocue/references/platform-rules.md).
9. **Two stories intercut.** **[source]** Cut only on matching beats: setup to setup, problem to problem, choice to choice. Each story must work alone first. Bridge them on a shared prop or action, which only works if that exists as its own close-up.

## Hooks

**[source]** The visual outcome drop-in is often named the strongest hook: zero setup, the payoff already in frame one. Nobody here has tested that.

- **Timing.** **[source]** Sources disagree, and this is the line: subject in frame 0, words by 1 second, hook readable by 3 seconds.
- **Speech early.** **[source]** One author counted dialogue in the first second, often with a number, on 18 of 21 of their own hits. One account, and no count of the misses.
- **Modest proof.** **[source]** The same author reports modest proof beating big claims. One account.
- **One scene per hook.** **[source]** Build each opener as a self-contained scene, and testing another one is a reorder of `segments` in `edl.json`, not a new shoot.

Openers that need no talking head:

- **Mid-action drop-in:** open inside the moment. Never establish first.
- **Wrong-scale object:** a tiny character and an absurdly big pile. Legible with no sound.
- **POV text overlay:** casts the viewer as the character.
- **Confession card:** one specific, slightly raw truth as text, which the scene answers. Specific beats clever.
- **Pattern break:** one discordant element in the world, resolved at the end.
- **Count promise:** "5 things" or "day 12" on frame one.

Never open with an establishing wide, a character waving hello, or any "let me tell you about" equivalent.

## Pacing

- **Pace to the content.** Measure with `altocue facts`, and read "Faster is not simply better" above.
- **Hold longer where evidence needs a beat.** **[source]** Give a proof time to be inspected.
- **Give a fast montage one slow move and one long still.** **[source]** The contrast is what makes the rest read as fast.
- **When one beat grows, name where the time comes from,** then recheck the rest. **[source]**
- **A long episode and its vertical cut are different films.** **[source]** If both are wanted, plan both from the start.

## Selling without showing the product

1. Dramatise the relief, not the mechanism.
2. The concept appears as the physics of the world: something that behaves the way the product behaves.
3. One concept per reel.
4. Fixed character registers. Bend the trend to the character, never the character to the trend.

## Never do

- Engagement-bait calls to action, such as "comment YES". **[source]** Platforms are said to suppress these.
- Proof made up for the film: case numbers, reviews, customer counts, prices. A placeholder never ships as a claim.
- A format with no new idea in it. See Batches in `altocue-direct`.

## Post text and calls to action

- **The post text is the second act.** The film makes the point. The text extends it in one line, with the words a viewer would search for.
- **Keep most reels free of a call to action.** Open questions are safe. Send traffic through the profile or a pinned comment.
