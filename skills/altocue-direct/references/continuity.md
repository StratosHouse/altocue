# Continuity: keeping a scene together across shots

Read this when a scene has more than one shot. Models have no memory between clips, so everything a film crew carries in their heads goes on the page. The labels are defined in [story.md](story.md).

## Before the first shot

- **Write a geography block, and open every prompt in the scene with it.** **[source]** Compass, who stands where, and each person's position in the first frame as a share of the screen. Several authors use this, and it is cheap.
- **Keep a timed ledger for props and damage.** **[source]** A table of what each person holds and what has happened to it, shot by shot. State only accumulates. A torn sleeve stays torn.
- **Keep separate sets for separate jobs.** **[source]** A twist gets its own room.
- **Plan the listening reaction as carefully as the line.** **[thumb]**
- **Put sound events in the same table as picture events.** **[source]** Both then ride one clock.

## Between shots

- **End each shot with a written end state, and open the next from it.** **[source]** Say where everyone is, what they hold and what just changed.
- **Chain clips last frame to first, then restart.** **[source]** Errors build, so go back to a fresh approved image after two or three links. Save a clip's last frame as a still, then point `first_frame` at it.
- **Carry direction across a cut.** **[source]** A move out runs into the next move in.

## The rules of the room

- **Stay on one side of the eyeline, and record screen direction for each shot.** **[thumb]** This is the 180 degree rule.
- **Light from sources that exist in the room, and keep them on the same side.** **[thumb]**
- **In speech-led pieces, change angle between sentences, not inside one.** **[source]**
- **When two generated people talk, keep each line inside one shot.** See [grammar.md](grammar.md).

## Check it

Read the shots in order for the ledger alone. Every state on screen agrees with every other frame: a counter, a prop, a name, a price, who holds what. **[source]** Nothing has happened before the film shows it happen.
